Case studies · WordPress · WooCommerce

Three storefronts for Bangladeshi retail

Seafood, general retail, and a cafe takeaway counter — each built on WordPress with the payment methods local customers actually use: bKash, Nagad, cards, and cash on delivery.

01

The sites

NoriBD — Seafood E-commerce

WordPress · WooCommerce · bKash / Nagad / cards · Cash on delivery

A seafood shop built around freshness and trust: product catalogue with weight and cut options, cart and checkout tuned for mobile, and delivery-area logic for Dhaka. Payment includes the Bangladeshi wallets plus cash on delivery, because a large share of customers will not prepay for perishable food.

Built with
WordPress, WooCommerce, custom theme work
Payments
bKash, Nagad, card, cash on delivery
Focus
Mobile-first catalogue and checkout

Bangu Bhozon — Multi-Category Retail

WordPress · WooCommerce · bKash / Nagad · Cash on delivery

A general retailer spanning gadgets, fashion, groceries, books and stationery, home essentials, and fresh seafood in one storefront. The work was mostly information architecture: category structure that stays navigable as the catalogue widens, shared product templates across unrelated departments, and checkout that does not ask a grocery buyer to fill in a form designed for electronics.

Built with
WordPress, WooCommerce, multi-category taxonomy
Departments
Six, including fresh seafood
Focus
Catalogue structure and checkout flow

Cafe Neptune — Takeaway Ordering

WordPress · WooCommerce · local payments

A takeaway counter for a cafe: a short menu that changes often, quick add-to-cart for repeat customers, and an order flow that assumes the customer is standing in the shop rather than browsing at a desk. Kept deliberately small — the menu is the product, and anything that slows ordering costs sales.

Built with
WordPress, WooCommerce
Model
Takeaway ordering
Focus
Speed of ordering
02

Live demo status

The public demos are offline. All three sites were developed on Pantheon staging hosts, and those environments have since been deleted — the URLs now return "unknown site". A WordPress storefront needs PHP and MySQL, which static hosting such as GitHub Pages cannot run, so a working copy cannot be published from this repository.

What exists is the full build: a complete WordPress export for each site, including the database, theme, plugins, and uploaded media. Restoring one takes about fifteen minutes and is documented step by step in IMPORT.md. The archives run 350–530 MB each and are available on request rather than committed here, because GitHub rejects files above 100 MB.
